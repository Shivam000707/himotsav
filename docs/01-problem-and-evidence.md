# 01 Problem and evidence

## The problem

Type 2 diabetes never goes away, so patients return to the doctor every few months for life. Over the years they collect prescriptions and test reports from many doctors and labs, in different formats, usually on loose paper. At each follow-up the doctor has only a few minutes to work out what the patient takes now, whether they miss doses, whether their blood sugar is improving, and whether complications such as kidney problems are developing. Information that is missing or jumbled can lead to missed tests, wrong assumptions about medicines, and rushed visits.

## Persona used throughout the project

**Ramesh ji, 58.** Diabetic for 10 years. Has seen 3 doctors and used 3 labs. Arrives with a bag of loose papers, one lab reporting sugar in mmol/L and another in mg/dL. He cannot recall every medicine. The persona is illustrative, not a real patient. The "about 5 minutes" per visit is also an illustration, not a measured figure.

## Evidence

| Claim | Number | Source | Verification status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size of the problem | An estimated **101 million** people in India have diabetes (11.4% prevalence; 113,043 adults, 31 states/UTs, 2008-2020) | ICMR-INDIAB, Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology, 2023 | Checked against press/summary reports of the paper. The original paper was not opened. It is a modelled estimate. |
| Records are thin | **24%** (122 of 509) of documents patients received at outpatient clinics contained diagnosis, medication, long-term care and follow-up information | Investigating clinical handover and healthcare communication for outpatients with chronic disease in India (PLoS ONE 2018, PMC6281223) | Checked in the full text. Covers chronic disease (diabetes, respiratory, cardiovascular, hypertension), **not diabetes only**. Kerala and Himachal Pradesh only. |
| Patients forget instructions | **55%** (283 of 513) recalled receiving verbal follow-up and medication instructions | Same study | Checked in the full text |
| Information systems are fragmented | Qualitative findings: fragmented information systems, little support for follow-up and self-management | BMC Health Services Research 2019 | Seen through search summaries only |

## How to quote these numbers

- Say "an estimated 101 million", not "101 million".
- Say the 24% and 55% are for chronic-disease outpatients in Kerala and Himachal Pradesh, not all of India and not diabetes only.
- Do not claim any time-saving figure until our own time study produces one (see [04 Evaluation plan](04-evaluation-plan.md)).

## Gap in existing tools (summary)

Tools already exist in India that read lab reports into a doctor's record system, take notes during consultations, or handle OPD registration. We did not find one that does a **patient-side, pre-consultation, diabetes-specific intake** with source-linked values. This comes from public web pages and press releases, not from testing those products, so present it as "we did not find", not as "none exist".
